\section{Qualitative and Diagnostic Analysis}















\subsection{Prediction-Level Case Analysis}

To further analyze the species-level improvement, this thesis compares the prediction outputs of the image-only H-CAST rerun and the proposed attribute-guided model on the CUB test set.

Table~\ref{tab:case_analysis_counts} summarizes the prediction-level case analysis. The proposed model corrects 236 species-level errors made by the H-CAST rerun. Among these corrected cases, 194 correspond to intra-family confusions, where H-CAST predicts the correct family but the wrong species. This accounts for approximately 82.2\% of the corrected species cases. This observation is consistent with the motivation of the proposed hard-sibling-aware attribute selection strategy, because many corrected errors occur between visually related species within the same family.

However, the improvement is not one-sided. The proposed model also introduces 161 new species-level errors where H-CAST was originally correct. This means that the attribute-guided branch changes the species-level decision boundary rather than simply adding correct predictions on top of the baseline. Nevertheless, the number of corrected errors is larger than the number of newly introduced errors, resulting in a net gain of 75 correctly classified species. Over 5,794 test images, this corresponds to approximately 1.29 percentage points, which is consistent with the observed species accuracy improvement from 84.55\% to 85.83\%.

At the same time, there remain 659 images where both models predict the wrong species. This indicates that the proposed attribute-guided module does not eliminate all fine-grained recognition errors. In addition, 86 images are cases where the proposed model predicts the correct species but the full hierarchical path is still inconsistent. This helps explain why the proposed model improves species accuracy and wAP, while its TICE is not the lowest among all ablation settings.

\subsection{Species-Level Attention Comparison}

In addition to prediction-level analysis, this thesis compares species-level attention maps between the image-only H-CAST rerun and the proposed attribute-guided model.

The maps in this analysis are computed from the species-stage class-token attention and back-projected to image superpixels using the H-CAST graph assignment. For each image, the two heatmaps are normalized using a shared scale, so the color intensity is comparable between H-CAST and the proposed model within the same row. However, the scale is computed independently for each image, so color intensity should not be compared across different rows. These maps are used only as qualitative diagnostic visualizations. They should not be interpreted as object localization, part localization, or attribute localization results.

Figure~\ref{fig:hcast_vs_attr_attention} shows representative corrected cases where the image-only H-CAST rerun predicts the wrong species while the proposed attribute-guided model predicts the correct species. In some examples, the two models assign stronger attention responses to different image regions, suggesting that attribute-guided learning can change the internal species-level attention pattern. However, this difference is not consistent across all corrected cases. In several examples, the two attention maps are visually similar, although the final species predictions are different.

This observation suggests that the improvement of the proposed model should not be explained mainly as a visually obvious shift of attention to different spatial regions. Attention maps provide only a two-dimensional diagnostic projection of model behavior. They indicate where stronger attention responses appear in the visualization, but they do not show the full high-dimensional feature representation used by the classifier. Therefore, two models may attend to similar bird regions while still encoding different semantic information and producing different species-level decisions.

Some examples also show attention responses on background or contextual regions. These responses should not be interpreted as localization failures, because localization is not an objective of this thesis. The model is trained for hierarchical classification and auxiliary attribute prediction, not for object, part, or attribute localization. In addition, H-CAST graph nodes are produced by visual grouping and may include both bird and background regions. Therefore, the maps are used only for qualitative diagnosis of model behavior.

The attribute-guided model is better understood at the representation and decision-boundary level. The CUB attributes used in this thesis are class-level training targets, not manually provided inputs at inference time. Therefore, they do not directly provide additional test-time information about attributes that are invisible due to pose, occlusion, distance, or viewpoint. Instead, they act as auxiliary semantic supervision during training. This supervision encourages the species-level branch to learn representations aligned with bird traits that are useful for fine-grained discrimination.

For this reason, the attention maps are not used as the main explanation for the performance improvement. The stronger evidence comes from the component ablations, prediction-level analysis, and inference-time attribute summary ablation. In particular, replacing the attribute summary vector with zeros or shuffling it across images substantially decreases species accuracy, while family and order accuracies remain nearly unchanged. This shows that the attribute-oriented representation is actually used by the species classifier. Therefore, the benefit of the proposed method is better explained by attribute-guided representation learning and species-level decision-boundary modification, rather than by explicit attribute localization or a consistently different spatial attention pattern.














\subsection{Discussion}

The qualitative and diagnostic analyses provide a more detailed view of the quantitative results. The prediction-level case analysis shows that the proposed model corrects more species-level errors than it introduces. Specifically, it corrects 236 species-level errors made by the H-CAST rerun, while introducing 161 new species-level errors where H-CAST was originally correct. This results in a net gain of 75 correctly classified test images. Over 5,794 test images, this corresponds to approximately 1.29 percentage points, which is consistent with the observed species accuracy improvement from 84.55\% to 85.83\%.

The analysis also shows that many of the corrected cases are intra-family confusions. Among the 236 corrected species-level errors, 194 cases correspond to images where H-CAST predicts the correct family but the wrong species. This accounts for approximately 82.2\% of the corrected cases. This observation is consistent with the motivation of the proposed hard-sibling-aware attribute selection strategy, which is designed to prioritize attributes that help distinguish visually related species within the same family.

However, the proposed method is not uniformly better than the image-only H-CAST baseline. Some predictions that are correct in H-CAST become incorrect in the proposed model, and 659 images remain misclassified by both models at the species level. This indicates that attribute-guided fusion changes the species-level decision boundary and can improve average performance without improving every individual prediction. Therefore, the proposed method should be interpreted as improving species-level recognition overall, especially for some intra-family confusions, rather than as a model that consistently corrects all fine-grained errors.

The diagnostic attention comparison should be interpreted carefully. It shows that the two models can produce different internal species-level attention patterns, but it does not provide a simple visual explanation for the performance gain. In some corrected cases, the attention maps of the two models differ visibly, while in other corrected cases they remain visually similar. This suggests that the improvement should not be explained mainly as a consistent spatial shift of attention to different image regions. This is not a limitation of the method, because attribute localization is not an objective of this thesis. The model is trained for hierarchical classification and auxiliary attribute prediction, not for pixel-level or part-level localization.

The main explanation for the improvement comes from the quantitative evidence. The fusion and attribute-supervision ablations show that direct species-level fusion can already improve over the image-only baseline, while auxiliary attribute supervision provides additional semantic guidance. The full cross-attention-based model with diversity regularization achieves the strongest species accuracy and wAP. The attribute-selection experiments further show that HSDAS-199 provides a targeted attribute supervision mask that performs well for species-level recognition.

The inference-time attribute summary ablation provides additional evidence that the attribute-oriented representation is used by the species classifier. When the attribute summary vector is replaced with zeros, species accuracy decreases from 85.83\% to 82.33\%, and FPA decreases from 84.36\% to 81.03\%. When the attribute summary is shuffled across images, species accuracy further decreases to 72.32\%, and FPA decreases to 71.23\%. In contrast, family and order accuracies remain almost unchanged because the attribute-guided fusion is applied only to the species-level branch. These results indicate that the attribute summary vector is not ignored by the species classifier and that it needs to correspond to the input image.

Together, the component ablations, inference-time intervention, and prediction-level case analysis suggest that attribute-guided learning improves H-CAST primarily by changing the species-level representation and decision boundary. The benefit should not be interpreted as evidence that the model explicitly localizes semantic attributes. Instead, the CUB attributes act as training-time semantic supervision that encourages the species branch to learn trait-oriented representations useful for fine-grained discrimination.

The path-consistency analysis also helps explain the trade-off observed in the quantitative results. There are 86 images where the proposed model predicts the correct species but the full hierarchical path is still inconsistent. This is consistent with the fact that the proposed model achieves strong species accuracy, FPA, and wAP in the main setting, but does not obtain the lowest TICE among all ablation settings. Since attribute-guided fusion is applied mainly to the species-level branch, it can improve fine-grained species discrimination without necessarily improving taxonomy consistency in every case.

Overall, the qualitative and diagnostic analyses support a cautious interpretation of the proposed method. Attribute-guided learning improves the main quantitative performance of H-CAST, especially species accuracy, FPA, and wAP, and many of the corrected errors are intra-family species confusions. However, the diagnostic attention comparison is included only to inspect model behavior. It is not used to prove attribute localization or to provide a complete causal explanation for the improvement.

\section{Discussion}

The proposed method studies whether semantic attribute guidance can improve fine-grained hierarchical bird classification. H-CAST provides a strong image-only hierarchical backbone by organizing image regions from fine to coarse and producing predictions at species, family, and order levels \cite{park2025visually}. However, in the CUB setting, it does not explicitly use the attribute annotations available in CUB-200-2011 \cite{wah2011cub}. This thesis investigates whether these attributes can be used as training-time auxiliary supervision to improve species-level discrimination while preserving image-only inference.

\subsection{Rationale of the Proposed Design}

Species-level bird classification often requires fine-grained semantic evidence. Two species from the same family may have similar global appearance, but differ in traits such as bill shape, wing pattern, crown color, breast markings, or tail shape. These traits are reflected in the CUB attribute annotations. Therefore, using attributes as auxiliary supervision is a natural way to encourage the model to encode visual cues that are relevant for species discrimination.

The proposed model attaches the attribute-guided module to the species-level branch because the species level is the finest and most difficult level in the hierarchy. Family and order classification are coarser tasks and may rely more on broader visual structures. For this reason, attribute-guided fusion is applied to the species-level branch, while family and order predictions remain produced by the original H-CAST hierarchical stages.

The use of attribute-group queries is motivated by the structure of the CUB attributes. Instead of predicting 312 independent binary attributes, the model operates on semantic attribute groups such as \textit{bill shape}, \textit{wing color}, \textit{head pattern}, and \textit{tail shape}. This group-wise formulation allows the auxiliary attribute task to follow the semantic organization of the CUB attributes.

HSDAS further improves the attribute usage strategy by selecting attributes according to species-level sibling discrimination. Using all 312 attributes may introduce redundancy or weakly useful supervision. HSDAS reduces this issue by prioritizing attributes that distinguish a species from its sibling species, especially hard siblings with similar attribute profiles. This makes the selected attribute subset more aligned with the fine-grained nature of the classification task.

\subsection{Advantages}

The first advantage of the proposed method is that it preserves image-only inference. Attribute annotations are used only during training to supervise the attribute branch. At test time, the model receives only an image and produces order, family, and species predictions. Therefore, the inference setting remains the same as the original image-only hierarchical backbone.

Second, the method is compatible with the official CUB-200-2011 train/test protocol. The model can be trained using the standard image labels and class-level attribute annotations provided by the dataset. No additional manual annotation, part localization label, or attribute input is required at inference time.

Third, the group-wise attribute design provides a semantically organized auxiliary task. Since each attribute head corresponds to a semantic group, the auxiliary supervision is aligned with human-interpretable bird traits such as bill shape, wing color, head pattern, and tail shape. This makes the attribute branch more structured than a single flat 312-dimensional prediction target.

Fourth, HSDAS provides a compact attribute subset compared with FullAttr-312. By selecting attributes that are useful for distinguishing sibling species, HSDAS is designed to reduce the attribute space while preserving discriminative semantic cues. This helps avoid using all attributes indiscriminately and provides a more task-specific attribute supervision mask.

Finally, the proposed framework allows controlled ablation of the main components, including attribute supervision, species-level fusion, cross-attention, diversity regularization, and attribute selection. This is important because performance gains may come from semantic supervision, additional capacity, or the interaction between different modules.

\subsection{Limitations}

Despite these advantages, the proposed method also has several limitations. First, it depends on the availability and quality of class-level attribute annotations. The approach is suitable for datasets such as CUB-200-2011, where attribute annotations are provided. However, it may not be directly applicable to datasets without reliable semantic attributes.

Second, the attribute targets used in this thesis are class-level attributes. Therefore, all images from the same species share the same attribute target, even though the visibility of a trait may vary across images due to pose, occlusion, viewpoint, or image quality. This makes the attribute supervision suitable for injecting species-level semantic priors, but less capable of modeling image-specific attribute presence.

Third, HSDAS is designed for settings where both a taxonomy and class-level attributes are available. The method relies on sibling relationships in the taxonomy and compares class-level attribute profiles to identify hard siblings. Therefore, its usefulness may be limited in datasets without a meaningful hierarchy or without class-level semantic descriptions.

Fourth, the current attribute fusion is applied mainly to the species-level branch. This design is reasonable for fine-grained bird recognition, but it means that family and order classifiers do not directly receive fused attribute representations. Future work could study whether attribute guidance at coarser levels can further improve hierarchical consistency.

Finally, the performance of the method may depend on the selected attribute mask and the loss weights. Different choices of \(\lambda_{attr}\), \(\lambda_{div}\), and the selected attribute subset can affect the balance between classification, attribute prediction, and diversity regularization. This makes ablation studies necessary to understand the contribution of each component. In particular, comparison with the direct-fusion baseline is needed to determine whether the benefit comes from attribute-query cross-attention or mainly from auxiliary attribute supervision and species-level fusion.

Overall, the proposed method extends H-CAST in a targeted way. It preserves the visually consistent hierarchical backbone, adds attribute-guided learning where fine-grained discrimination is most needed, and uses HSDAS to select a compact set of discriminative attributes. The method is therefore well suited to the CUB-200-2011 setting, where species recognition depends on subtle bird traits and where both taxonomy and attribute annotations are available.

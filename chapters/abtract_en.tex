\textbf{\large{Abstract}}
\end{center}

\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\begin{small}

Fine-grained bird image classification is challenging because many visually similar species differ only in subtle traits such as bill shape, wing pattern, head pattern, breast color, crown color, or tail shape. The task becomes more difficult in a hierarchical setting, where a model must predict not only the fine-grained species label but also higher taxonomic levels such as family and order. Recent prompt-based and language-assisted fine-grained recognition methods suggest that semantic visual traits can serve as useful intermediate evidence when direct species recognition is difficult. Motivated by this observation, this thesis investigates whether CUB visual attributes can be used as training-time semantic supervision to improve image-only hierarchical bird classification.

This thesis proposes an attribute-guided extension of H-CAST for CUB-200-2011. The proposed method preserves H-CAST as the image-based hierarchical backbone and adds an attribute-guided module to the species-level branch, where fine-grained discrimination is most difficult. CUB attributes are organized into semantic groups, and each active group is supervised by a group-specific attribute classifier. Attribute-oriented features are extracted from species-level graph-node representations and fused with the species class token before final species classification. During inference, the model receives only the input image and does not require manually provided attribute labels. This thesis also studies Hard-Sibling Discriminative Attribute Selection (HSDAS), which selects attributes that help distinguish visually similar sibling species within the same family. In addition, direct-fusion alternatives and diversity regularization are evaluated to analyze the contribution of different components.

Experiments are conducted on the official CUB-200-2011 train/test split. Compared with the H-CAST rerun, the proposed attribute-guided H-CAST improves Full-Path Accuracy from 82.86\% to 84.36\%, species accuracy from 84.55\% to 85.83\%, family accuracy from 95.22\% to 95.62\%, order accuracy from 98.74\% to 99.03\%, and weighted Average Precision from 86.90\% to 88.00\%. Tree-based InConsistency Error is also reduced from 4.35\% to 3.62\%. Ablation studies show that species-level fusion, auxiliary attribute supervision, attribute selection, and diversity regularization all affect the final performance. Direct-fusion results indicate that additional species-level fusion capacity already contributes to the improvement, while attribute supervision provides further semantic guidance. Inference-time ablation further shows that replacing or shuffling the attribute summary vector substantially degrades species accuracy, indicating that the species classifier actually uses the attribute-oriented representation.

Prediction-level analysis shows that the proposed model corrects 236 species-level errors made by the H-CAST rerun, including 194 intra-family species confusions. This supports the motivation that attribute guidance is useful for distinguishing visually similar species within the same family. Diagnostic attention analysis shows that the performance improvement should not be interpreted as evidence of precise attribute localization. Instead, the benefit is better understood as attribute-guided representation learning and species-level decision-boundary modification.

\textbf{Keywords}: Fine-grained visual classification, hierarchical image classification, bird species recognition, visual attributes, H-CAST, attribute-guided learning.

\end{small}
